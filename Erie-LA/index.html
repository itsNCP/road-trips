Erie, CO → Los Angeles 3 days · I-25 / I-40 · Tesla + dog · Day 1 & 2 depart 9am, Day 3 depart 8am
Heads upArizona doesn't observe Daylight Saving Time. In August it shares clock time with California/Pacific — so once you cross into AZ on Day 2, you gain an hour back versus Colorado/New Mexico time.
This guide flexes with youDay 1 and Day 2 endpoints below are baselines, not fixed stops. Charging stops are fixed (you need those regardless), but where you actually stop for the night is your call on the road — see the tiered options at the end of each day, and the full Overnight tab for hotel picks at every realistic stopping point from Albuquerque all the way to Barstow.
Monsoon watch — general rules for both Day 1 and Day 2
  • Storms build through the afternoon and typically peak 2–6pm, then clear by evening — an early start and pushing to arrive before mid-afternoon is the single best way to dodge them on any given day.
  • This isn't just an AZ thing — the I-25 stretch through Raton Pass/northern NM sees summer storms too, not only the Flagstaff high country on Day 2.
  • Flash flooding is the real danger, not just rain — washes and low dips flood fast and can undercut the road. Never drive through flowing water; if a wash is running, wait it out.
  • If you hear thunder on an exposed overlook or trail (most of Petrified Forest counts), head back to the car — there's no natural shelter out there.
  • Quick check each morning before you leave: a glance at the NWS forecast or a weather app's radar for the day's route takes 30 seconds and beats guessing.

Where to stop tonight — the big lever for Day 3
Where is the hottest part of the morning?Needles, CA is the single hottest point on this entire route — it routinely posts the nation's hottest daily temperature and runs consistently hotter than Barstow (average August highs ~103–108°F vs. Barstow's ~97–100°F). Both Needles and Barstow are already climbing fast by 9–10am. The single biggest heat-avoidance move on this whole trip is pushing your Day 2 stop past Kingman and getting through Needles and into the Barstow/Victorville area at night, while it's still 80s–90s instead of 100s+. Do that, and Day 3 is just a short, cool morning hop into LA.

This is the move if you want the shortest, easiest Day 3. You'd be driving the Kingman→Needles stretch in the evening/night instead of mid-morning heat — genuinely more pleasant and safer than doing it in triple-digit daytime temps. Barstow's Venner Park (4.6★) is solid too, if you'd rather not push quite that far. Arrive late, but Day 3 becomes a short, cool morning hop into LA. Long Day 2 though — be honest with yourself about fatigue after a few hours on your feet at Petrified Forest.

Day 3 · Wednesday Aug 12→ Los Angeles, CA. Start point and time depend on where you stopped Day 2.

If you stopped in Flagstaff or Williams~480 mi to go — this is the full desert-crossing day. Depart by 8am so the Kingman→Needles→Barstow stretch happens in the morning instead of mid-afternoon. Straight through: Kingman ~10:15am, Needles/Barstow area ~12:30–1pm, LA by mid-afternoon. Being honest — an 8am start won't make the low desert actually cool (it's already climbing by 10am in August), but it gets you through the worst of it 3–4 hours earlier than a later departure would.
If you stopped in Kingman~330 mi to go. Skip straight to the Needles/Barstow charging stop below — still worth an early-ish start (8–9am) to clear Needles before late morning, but you've already cut the day meaningfully.
If you stopped in Needles or Barstow~150–230 mi to go. You already did the hard part at night — this is a short, easy morning drive. No need to rush an early departure; a normal 8–9am start still gets you into LA before lunch.
WeatherFlagstaff/Williams start cool and clear (~60–70°F). Kingman/Needles/Barstow: 100–110°F by midday, sunny, dry — Needles runs hottest. LA arrival is mild and pleasant, low-to-mid 80s. AC and hydration matter more than anything else today.

Arrival

Los Angeles

Getting through Cajon Pass / I-15 before mid-afternoon should also mean lighter traffic than a typical later arrival — one more reason the earlier/further Day 2 options pay off.

How this park worksYou enter at the north end (I-40 exit 311, Painted Desert Visitor Center) and exit at the south end (US-180 near Holbrook) via a single 28-mile road — no backtracking, no loop. Everything below is listed in driving order, north to south. Leashed dogs are allowed on every trail and overlook in the park, just not inside buildings. Pick 3–5 stops rather than all of them if you want to keep it to a few hours.
Shade reality checkThis is high desert badlands at ~5,400 ft — there are almost no trees. "Cooler" here means: the two visitor centers (real A/C), the Painted Desert Inn's shaded porch, and the two spots below with an actual covered picnic shelter. Everything else is open sky. Sun shirts/hats and water for both of you matter more than shade-hunting.

Start here · Indoor / A·C

Right at the north entrance off I-40

Tawa Point & Kachina Point overlooks

Short paved paths right off the road, a few minutes each

Painted Desert Inn

Historic Fred Harvey building, National Historic Landmark, 9am–4pm

The single best photo stop in the park — the view from the inn's overlook is the classic shot. Covered porch and an air-conditioned interior make this the best mid-visit break for both of you. Dogs aren't allowed inside, but the porch and overlook are outside. Ask about the Bark Ranger program if you want to make it official for the dog.

Newspaper Rock overlook

Roadside pullout, scopes provided

Dense cluster of petroglyphs viewed from an overlook — quick stop, don't even need to get the dog out.

Middle of the park

Easy walk ★ · 1 mi paved loop · Shaded picnic shelter

Blue Mesa Trail

3rd/4th pullout on the Blue Mesa spur road — look for the covered picnic table

The best all-around stop in the park and one of the only ones with an actual covered picnic shelter at the trailhead. The paved loop descends ~100 ft into a valley of striped blue/purple badland hills, which also means partial relief from direct sun and wind versus the mesa top. Best photo light is morning. Easy grade, dog-friendly, 30–60 min.

Photo · No walk needed

Agate Bridge

Roadside overlook

A 110-ft petrified log spanning a small wash, slowly eroding out from under its supports. Quick look from the overlook.

Easy walk · 0.8 mi loop

Crystal Forest

Paved loop

Dense, close-up concentration of crystal-flecked petrified wood right along the path. No shade. One of the better photo trails for the wood itself rather than the landscape.

South entrance — Rainbow Forest

Indoor / A·C · good lunch break

Rainbow Forest Museum & Visitor Center

At the south entrance

Fossil and geology exhibits, restrooms, gift shop, A/C. Good spot for a real break before you exit onto US-180.

Easy walk · 0.4–0.5 mi paved loop

Giant Logs Trail

Right behind the museum

Shortest, easiest walk in the park and dog-friendly — the largest, most colorful logs in the park, including the 10-ft-wide "Old Faithful." Limited shade but right next to the A/C museum for a break before or after.

If you want more walkingLong Logs + Agate House

2.6 mi round trip combined, also from the Rainbow Forest parking lot. Long Logs has one of the densest concentrations of petrified wood in the park; Agate House is a small reconstructed pueblo built from petrified wood. Essentially no shade for the whole loop except one small shelter at the 0.5-mile trail split — only worth it if you're ahead of schedule and it's not yet peak heat.

Setup — once before you leave

Setup

Hips level with or just above knees

Raise seat height until you look slightly down at the wheel. Knees bent 20–30° — never locked straight, that strains the joint.

Setup

Tailbone all the way back

Scoot flush into the seat crease before adjusting anything else. No gap between low back and seat back.

Setup

Recline to 100–110°

Not bolt upright — 90° actually loads the discs more than a slight recline does.

Setup

Lumbar support at belt line

Fill the low-back curve with built-in support or a rolled towel. No pressure point, no gap.

Setup

Wheel 10–12" from chest, hands 9 & 3

Elbows softly bent. Set mirrors after the seat — if you have to lean to see them, posture already slipped.

Setup

Headrest close, not just high

The back of your head should rest against it in normal posture, not just clear the top — that's what actually protects your neck.

Knee noteKeep a two-finger gap between the front of the seat and the back of your knee, so the cushion isn't pressing into the knee pit on a long drive.

Restlessness playbook Anchor breaks to Superchargers — you're already stopping every 1.5–2 hrs, that's your built-in movement cadence. Set a 20-min silent "wiggle" chime for the in-seat moves above. Keep hands busy safely: textured wheel cover, chewing gum. Let the dog's walk be your walk too — same stop, same grass, double reset. If focus is drifting, change the music genre entirely rather than skipping tracks — a full reset works better than fiddling. Counting mile markers or naming things you see (colors, animals, plates) for a minute or two gives the restless part of your brain something concrete to do without needing a stop.
